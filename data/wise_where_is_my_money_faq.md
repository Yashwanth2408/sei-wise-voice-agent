# Wise FAQ Scope: Where is my money?

This knowledge base is intentionally limited to the Wise "Where is my money?" section only.
The voice agent must answer only questions grounded in this document.
If the user asks anything outside this scope, the agent must deflect to a human agent and end the call.

## Allowed Topics

- How to check a transfer status in Wise
- Understanding transfer tracker statuses
- Why a transfer may be delayed
- What the sender can do to speed things up
- When money should arrive
- Why Wise may show complete before the recipient sees the money
- Why the transfer is taking longer than the estimate
- Proof of payment
- What proof of payment must include
- Banking partner reference numbers

## Transfer Status Tracking

Customers can check transfer status in the Wise account tracker:
1. Log in to Wise
2. Go to Home
3. Open the relevant transfer

Common tracker statuses:
- "Your money's being processed": Wise is still waiting to receive the money from the sender's bank.
- "Money received": Wise has received the money and is converting it.
- "Transfer sent": Wise has sent the money to the recipient's bank. The recipient bank may still need a few working days to process it.
- "Complete": Wise has sent the money to the recipient bank, but the bank may still be processing or the recipient may not recognize the transfer.

## Delay Reasons

Common reasons a transfer may be delayed:
- The sender's bank has not yet sent the money to Wise
- Wise needs extra information for verification or compliance checks
- Recipient details may be incorrect
- Weekend or public holiday processing delays
- The recipient bank is still processing the payout
- The chosen payment method is slower than others

## Payment Method Timelines

Typical time for money to reach Wise:
- Card payments: usually instant
- Bank transfers: 1 to 4 working days
- Swift transfers: 1 to 6 working days

## Recipient Detail Issues

Minor typos in names may not always cause a problem, but some currencies can be stricter.
If an account number or important banking detail is wrong:
- the transfer may be delayed
- the bank may reject it
- the money may be returned to Wise

## Weekends And Holidays

Banks usually do not process transfers on weekends or public holidays.
Some banks may also stop processing early on Fridays.
This can delay arrival until the next working day.

## If The Recipient Says The Money Has Not Arrived

Ask them to check:
- the sender name on the statement, which may show Wise or a Wise partner instead of the original sender
- the amount and reference number
- the recipient bank details
- whether the account currency differs from the sent currency

A transfer receipt can help the recipient's bank locate the transfer.

## Transfer Receipt

The sender can download a transfer receipt from the Wise account and share it with the recipient.
The receipt includes payment details and may include tracking numbers for some currencies.
This helps the recipient bank trace the transfer.

## Security Checks

Wise may perform extra checks for safety and compliance.
If Wise needs more information, it will email the customer.
These checks cannot be sped up manually.

## Proof Of Payment

Wise may ask for proof of payment when:
- the money has not reached Wise in the expected time
- Wise needs to verify the payment came from an account in the sender's name

Accepted proof of payment may include:
- a PDF bank statement
- a high-quality screenshot from online banking

It must clearly show:
- sender full name and account number
- sender bank name
- Wise name and account number
- payment initiation date
- amount
- currency
- reference

Additional notes:
- two screenshots are acceptable if both show the same bank account number
- copied text in email is not acceptable
- a photo of a computer screen is not acceptable
- Swift payments require a pacs.008 document
- Australia and New Zealand payments may also require a recent bank statement

## Banking Partner Reference Number

Some transfers include a banking partner reference number.
The recipient can give this number to their bank to help trace the transfer.
In India, this may be called a UTR or transaction reference number.

## Out Of Scope Topics

The following are out of scope and must be deflected:
- new transfers
- fees
- exchange rates
- account opening
- KYC beyond transfer-delay verification context
- card issues
- refunds not directly tied to the tracked transfer delay flow
- disputes, fraud claims, chargebacks
- changing recipient details as an action workflow
- closing accounts
- anything not clearly grounded in the FAQ above
